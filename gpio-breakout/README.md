# koyomi gpio-breakout A0 — 40-pin FFC to Pi GPIO header

Bench adapter: the carrier/LCD system's 40-contact FFC (Hirose
FH41-40S-0.5SH(05), C596805 — the JDISP1/JFFC1 interconnect) broken out 1:1 to
a standard Raspberry Pi 40-pin 2.54 mm header. Lets a stock Pi drive the
Koyomi LVDS hat + panel chain over the production FFC before any carrier
exists — or probes a carrier's display FFC from a known-good header.

Board: 63.5 x 30 mm, 2-layer, GND pours both sides. ERC 0, DRC 0/0/0, fully
routed (generated headlessly; tools/ carries the generators; routed with
Freerouting 2.2.4, jar SHA-256 verified against the carrier repo's recorded
hash).

## The three special pins — jumpers OPEN by default

Pins 17/34/39 are 3V3/GND/GND on a real Pi but PANEL_ID0/PANEL_ID1/LCD_INS in
the FFC system (interconnect contract, carrier-lcd). A hard-wired adapter
would tie the LCD's ID straps to power rails — the exact hazard flagged
against carrier A3.8.7. So they are ISOLATED here, with solder jumpers and
test pads:

| FFC pin | Net | JP | Close it to... | Default |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 17 | PANEL_ID0 | JP1 -> 3V3 | strap ID0 high | OPEN |
| 34 | PANEL_ID1 | JP2 -> GND | strap ID1 low | OPEN |
| 39 | LCD_INS | JP3 -> GND | report "panel inserted" | OPEN |

TP1-TP3 expose the three FFC-side nets; TP4/TP5 give GND/3V3 for flying
straps. The retrofit ignores the straps entirely (WP-003e), so OPEN is correct
for driving the Koyomi chain; close or fly-wire only for experiments that
read/emulate the straps.

## Fit notes

- J2 accepts a 2x20 stacking socket on the BOTTOM face to ride a Pi like a
  hat, or a pin header on top for jumper cables. Pin 1 is the square pad.
- All other 37 contacts are straight-through pin-N-to-pin-N; the FFC is
  bottom-contact — same cable/fold rules as the carrier-LCD link.
- DPI on GPIO0-21 at 3.3 V only, as everywhere in this system.
